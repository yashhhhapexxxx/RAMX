# Why RAMX fits Autonomous Business

A college student's laptop sits idle for eight hours every night, and its memory is half empty most of the day. RAMX turns that idle machine into a small business that runs while its owner sleeps. The student joins a GPU Pool once, leaves RAMX running and goes to bed; overnight the laptop holds its slice of an AI model, serves requests from people and AI agents around the world, and gets paid for every answer it helps produce. It earns two ways. It earns **money**: a share of what every customer pays, settled to the student's own account each payout period. And it earns **compute**: credits for the same AI models, so the work the laptop does at night pays for the student's own AI use the next day, and the more they share, the further up the queue they move, like a good seeder in BitTorrent. No subscription, no GPU rental, no new hardware: the laptop they already own pays for their AI, and then some.

The business runs itself, and nobody has to trust anyone. Prices adjust on their own every 30 seconds with demand, cheaper when capacity is idle to attract customers and higher when it is busy, so a quiet night still fills up and a busy one earns more. Earnings are split automatically by how much of the model each laptop served, providers get a bonus just for being online and ready, and each payout period settles on its own with no invoices and no manual payroll. The rules protect the network from its own members: earnings vest for an hour and are taken back if a laptop is caught cheating, customers are billed only for what was actually delivered, their emails, phone numbers and card numbers are hidden from the laptops that serve them, and a reserve is set aside from every payment. Every one of these money movements is signed and recorded on a public blockchain, so a student can wake up, see exactly what their laptop earned overnight and verify it themselves. Millions of idle laptops become one open, self-running AI business owned by the people who power it.
