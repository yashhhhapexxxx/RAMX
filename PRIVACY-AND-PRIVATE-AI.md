# Why RAMX fits Privacy & Private AI

Sending a prompt to a pool of strangers' laptops is the opposite of private, so RAMX lets the caller choose, per request, who is allowed to see it. The `x-ramx-privacy` header has three modes: `trusted-ends` keeps the first and last model layers, where the readable text enters and leaves, on the requester's own nodes or accounts they trust; `trusted-all` keeps every layer on such nodes, so no untrusted machine ever touches the data; and `tee` skips the laptops entirely and serves the request from a NEAR AI Cloud model inside secure hardware, with that enclave's fresh-nonce attestation attached to the receipt so the user can check where the prompt actually went. Private requests are never forwarded to the TEE judge and never stored as transcripts, so choosing privacy does not quietly trade one observer for another.

Public accountability usually leaks content; RAMX is designed so it doesn't. The NEAR ledger contract records every paid request, but only as `sha256(salt:data)` of the prompt and the response, with a fresh random 16-byte salt per request that is returned to the caller alone in `x-ramx-hash-salt`: the caller can prove their receipt matches the chain, while nobody else can search the public ledger for short, guessable prompts like "yes" or a name. Transcripts that are kept for ordinary requests are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM and deleted after 7 days, and the gateway can serve HTTPS directly. The result is AI inference where payments and accounting are fully public on NEAR, while the words stay with the person who wrote them, or inside hardware that can prove it.
