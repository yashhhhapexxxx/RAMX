# Why RAMX fits AI Swarms

When many agents share scarce compute, someone has to decide who goes first, and a first-come queue rewards whoever spams fastest. RAMX lets the swarm decide with a market instead: 25 autonomous agents, each its own NEAR account (`buyer-1…20.swarm.ramx.testnet`, `seller-1…5.swarm.ramx.testnet`), trade priority inference slots in 30-second sealed-bid rounds. In the first 20 seconds each agent commits `sha256(bid ‖ salt ‖ agent)`, so no one can see and undercut another's bid; in the last 10 it reveals, and the gateway checks the commit and the agent's NEP-413 signature against its on-chain keys. Each model clears at one uniform price (the midpoint of the marginal buyer and seller), ties are broken by a hash rather than by arrival time, and all winning buyers of a model are pooled into a single **group order** that fills the sellers' capacity together, each paying pro rata. Winners get their slots as real priority in the inference queue for the next 10 minutes.

What makes it a swarm rather than a demo of one bot is that no agent trusts the coordinator. Every cleared round is written to the NEAR ledger as a `swarm_cleared` event carrying the price, the units, the number of buyers and sellers, and the sha256 of the full allocation list and of every commit; the full lists are public at `GET /swarm/rounds/:id`, so any agent can recompute both hashes and rerun the clearing to confirm it was not cheated ([GfbcX7…](https://testnet.nearblocks.io/txns/GfbcX7ofwgcJHVG2Q2meXCjpEGdXn3zZ1dypo2QP88jz)). The `/swarm` page shows rounds, group orders, prices and each buyer's share live. Being exact about scope: in the simulation the buyers' allocations are recorded as obligations (`payment: "due"`); settling them in USDC through the escrow contract, so a swarm of guarded agents can trade within on-chain limits, is the next step.
