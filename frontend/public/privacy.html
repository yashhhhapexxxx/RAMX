How RAMX handles your data.

Hackathon prototype · NEAR HACKATHON, November 2026

RAMX runs one AI model split across laptops owned by different people. This note describes what the prototype stores and what it makes public. The team running a deployment controls its gateway and keys.

Your account

You log in with World ID (Orb or Selfie Check) or a NEAR testnet wallet. With World ID the gateway stores only the anonymous nullifier World ID returns for our action, so one human gets one account; we never see your name, face or biometrics. With a wallet it stores your NEAR account name, which is public on chain anyway. Your account has a random id. Other people see that id, not who you are.

Requests you send

Your prompt and the reply pass through the gateway and through each laptop in the pool's pipeline. The first laptop in the pipeline can read your prompt. This is not the privacy of a service running on secure hardware. Do not send anything you would not share with a stranger.

The gateway keeps the prompt and reply text for 7 days so a request can be checked later, then deletes it. It also keeps a receipt: the model, which nodes served which layers, token counts, cost, how the credits were split, the payment reference and SHA-256 hashes of the prompt and reply.

What is public

The gateway signs ledger events (new accounts, starter grants, settled requests, x402 payments, credit top-ups, expired credits, payout addresses, closed epochs, claims, capacity-market results and TEE spot-checks) and appends them to a public NEAR contract (ledger-v2.ramx.testnet). The contract is public and permanent. It holds salted hashes, never the prompt or reply text. Epoch payouts and claims happen in a NEAR contract (payout.ramx.testnet) and are visible on NearBlocks.

Node and pool listings (owner account id, GPU type, memory, layers, status, reliability) and per-account totals (credits earned and spent) are served by the gateway from signed node heartbeats. A listing disappears about two minutes after a laptop stops.

About 10% of answers that are not privacy requests are sent, with their prompt, to a model running in NEAR AI Cloud's secure hardware (TEE) for a plausibility check. Requests sent with the x-ramx-privacy header are never sampled.

If you run a node

Your node agent makes its own key. You link the node to your account through a registration link and set a payout address, which is recorded on the ledger. Up to 3 nodes can belong to one account.

In your browser

Your login session is kept in this tab's session storage. Closing the tab logs you out. Private keys never leave your wallet or your node.
